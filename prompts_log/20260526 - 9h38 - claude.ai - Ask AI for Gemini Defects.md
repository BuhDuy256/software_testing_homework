
### 3. Google Gemini Image Generation Racial Bias (February 2024)

**Source:** NPR - https://www.npr.org/2024/02/28/1234532775/google-gemini-offended-users-images-race

**Description:** Shortly after Google added image-generation capabilities to Gemini (formerly Bard), users reported severe historical inaccuracies caused by an over-correction for diversity bias. When prompted to generate images of historical groups - U.S. Founding Fathers, 1943 German soldiers, medieval English kings, Viking warriors - the model consistently substituted racially diverse figures where historical context called for specific demographics. The system also refused to generate images of "white people" while fulfilling similar requests for other racial groups, exposing an asymmetric bias in its training and RLHF fine-tuning.

**Solution:** Google suspended image generation of people while conducting an extensive audit of training data and RLHF reward signals. Re-introduction of the feature was phased, with revised guidelines requiring historically accurate outputs and clearer handling of demographic prompts.

A few things worth clarifying upfront, since the framing of your question matters here.

The images weren't "racist" in the traditional sense - the problem was essentially the opposite: an over-correction for diversity that produced historically inaccurate outputs. America's Founding Fathers were depicted as Black women, and Ancient Greek warriors as Asian men and women. This was jarring and wrong, but the underlying cause was an aggressive diversity intervention gone too far, not a system designed to demean any group. 
Al Jazeera

What actually caused it?

There were two interlocking technical issues:

1. A blanket diversity prompt injection

Google appears to have added hidden instructions to user prompts to ensure diverse results. A prompt like "generate an image of a programmer" would effectively be rewritten as "generate an image of a programmer keeping diversity in mind." This universal diversity-specific instruction was applied before generating images of people, which led to the problems - even when generating images of historically specific groups like 1943 German soldiers or Viking warriors. 
Beebom

2. RLHF fine-tuning shaped by annotator demographics and instructions

Researchers pointed to Google's training process as the likely root cause. RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback) involves people telling the model what outputs are "better" or "worse," which in practice shapes the model's reward function. Depending on who Google recruited as annotators and what instructions they were given, this could have reinforced the pattern of over-diversifying all image outputs. 
Outlook India

3. Tuning rules lacking historical context

Some of the post-training tuning rules were generalized without context, causing inaccurate portrayals of historically specific groups like Vikings or Nazi soldiers. Researchers argued the system had effectively sacrificed historical accuracy for numerical diversity metrics. 
Aicerts News

What about the asymmetric behavior?

The broader problem with AI image generators is that they have a long-standing tendency to reproduce stereotypes - early systems overwhelmingly defaulted to white male figures in professional roles. Attempts to correct this embedded bias have often backfired, either failing to generate diverse subjects or including diversity where it would be contextually inappropriate. Google's diversity correction was a response to that real, documented problem - it just swung too far in the other direction without drawing a distinction between open-ended prompts (where diversity is appropriate) and historically specific prompts (where accuracy should take precedence). 
Brookings

On the "internal training data" question

It's important to note that Google has not publicly disclosed the specific training datasets used for Gemini's image generation. AI image generators generally train on large internet-scraped datasets, which can contain biased content - but a lack of diversity in who uploads training data can also cause the AI to learn skewed visual patterns. The Gemini issue, however, was less about the raw training data and more about the post-training interventions - specifically the RLHF reward signals and prompt-injection diversity rules that were applied on top of the base model.
