#### 1. Google Bard Hallucination - JWST Factual Error (February 2023)

**Source:** CNN Business - https://www.cnn.com/2023/02/08/tech/google-ai-bard-demo-error

**Description:** In a promotional video published on 6 February 2023, Google's Bard chatbot (now Gemini) was asked what new discoveries from the James Webb Space Telescope it could explain to a child. Bard stated that JWST "took the very first pictures of a planet outside of our own solar system." That claim was factually incorrect: the first direct image of an exoplanet was captured by the European Southern Observatory's Very Large Telescope in 2004 - 17 years before JWST launched. Reuters identified the error on the same day as a major Google AI event in Paris.

**Type:** AI/LLM

**Severity:** Critical (business and reputational)

**Consequences:** Alphabet's share price fell approximately 7.7% within hours, removing roughly **$100 billion** in market capitalisation. The incident raised serious doubts about the reliability of Bard before it had even launched publicly and gave a significant PR advantage to OpenAI's ChatGPT. It became the defining early example of LLM hallucination in a high-stakes commercial context.

**Solution:** Google delayed the public release of Bard to add further fact-checking safeguards, conducted additional internal red-teaming, and later added real-time web grounding to reduce factual errors. The model was subsequently renamed Gemini with improved accuracy benchmarks.

#### 2. ChatGPT Fabricated Legal Citations - *Mata v. Avianca* (May 2023)

**Source:** MIT Sloan Teaching & Learning Technologies - https://mitsloanedtech.mit.edu/ai/basics/addressing-ai-hallucinations-and-bias/

**Description:** In a personal injury lawsuit filed in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, attorneys Steven Schwartz and Peter LoDuca submitted a legal brief containing six case citations generated by ChatGPT. None of the cases existed. The fabricated citations included fake case names, fake docket numbers, and fake legal reasoning. When the attorneys asked ChatGPT to verify the citations, the model confirmed they were real and claimable on LexisNexis and Westlaw - a second-order hallucination.

**Type:** AI/LLM

**Severity:** Critical

**Consequences:** Federal Judge P. Kevin Castel found the lawyers had acted with "subjective bad faith" and sanctioned both attorneys and their firm **$5,000** under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 11. The case became the most widely cited example of AI hallucination risk in professional settings worldwide and prompted guidance from bar associations across multiple jurisdictions.

**Solution:** Courts in multiple countries issued standing orders requiring disclosure when AI tools are used to prepare legal filings. Law firms introduced mandatory AI-verification policies, and OpenAI added more prominent disclaimers recommending independent verification of all factual claims.

---

#### 3. Google Gemini Image Generation Racial Bias (February 2024)

**Source:** NPR - https://www.npr.org/2024/02/28/1234532775/google-gemini-offended-users-images-race

**Description:** Shortly after Google added image-generation capabilities to Gemini (formerly Bard), users reported severe historical inaccuracies caused by an over-correction for diversity bias. When prompted to generate images of historical groups - U.S. Founding Fathers, 1943 German soldiers, medieval English kings, Viking warriors - the model consistently substituted racially diverse figures where historical context called for specific demographics. The system also refused to generate images of "white people" while fulfilling similar requests for other racial groups, exposing an asymmetric bias in its training and RLHF fine-tuning.

**Type:** AI/LLM

**Severity:** Critical (trust, bias, and accuracy)

**Consequences:** Google's CEO Sundar Pichai publicly apologised, stating the results "offended our users." Google suspended Gemini's image-generation-of-people feature entirely in February 2024 - an unprecedented rollback for a major product feature. Alphabet's stock fell further. The incident renewed global debate about how AI developers address bias and whether diversity-driven fine-tuning can unintentionally introduce new biases.

**Solution:** Google suspended image generation of people while conducting an extensive audit of training data and RLHF reward signals. Re-introduction of the feature was phased, with revised guidelines requiring historically accurate outputs and clearer handling of demographic prompts.

---

#### 4. Bing Chat / ChatGPT Prompt Injection - System Prompt Leak (February–December 2023)

**Source:** Wikipedia - Prompt Injection - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prompt_injection

**Description:** In February 2023, a Stanford student demonstrated that Microsoft's Bing Chat (powered by GPT-4) could be manipulated via direct prompt injection to ignore its prior instructions, revealing internal hidden system directives and its codename "Sydney." Another researcher verified the exploit by posing as an OpenAI developer. Separately, in December 2024, The Guardian reported that OpenAI's ChatGPT Search tool was vulnerable to indirect prompt injection: malicious content embedded in webpages could alter ChatGPT's summarised responses to users - without the user or the model detecting the attack.

**Type:** AI/LLM

**Severity:** High
